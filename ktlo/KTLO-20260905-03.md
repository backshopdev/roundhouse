## Status

## Description

The orchestrator's standing role is to "plan and route work through
`document-author`, `implementor`, and independently composed `reviewer`
sessions" (per `src/.opencode/agents/orchestrator.md`). The role
description is silent on *how* the orchestrator should interact with
the human collaborator when the human is working in areas of limited
knowledge — for example: debugging an unfamiliar failure mode,
evaluating an unfamiliar design decision, or asking the orchestrator
to "explain why" something happened.

The broader intent is to build a workflow that helps both human and
agent contributors grow. The agent-side growth loop (better
documentation, memory systems, captured domain knowledge) is partly
out of scope for this KTLO. This KTLO focuses on the human-side
growth loop: when the human is learning, the orchestrator should
adopt a posture that supports that learning, not just delivers
answers. Anything developed here that turns out to also serve
agent contributors should be noted but isn't the primary target.

Within the human-side growth loop, the observation underlying this
KTLO: when the human and the agent collaborate well, each fills
gaps the other can't. The human carries undocumented domain
knowledge (project history, intent, prior decisions, constraints
not yet written down). The agent carries consistency, repeatability,
and the ability to articulate reasoning explicitly. In the
orchestrator's case, surfacing the undocumented knowledge and
capturing it for the project is part of the handoff — the human
teaches the orchestrator about the project's lived reality, and
the orchestrator captures it (via decisions, specs, plans,
updates to working docs, KTLOs, etc.) so future agents inherit it.

In practice, the observed behavior has been: the orchestrator
explains its reasoning step by step when debugging or evaluating
options, names the model/assumption being applied, surfaces
tradeoffs, and flags adjacent concerns. This has worked well in
the 0.6.0 release work (e.g., the scratch/README debugging, the
Node version discussion, the em-dash corruption diagnosis). The
intent of this KTLO is to make that posture deliberate and durable
rather than ad hoc.

The principle: **when the human is working in an area of limited
knowledge, the orchestrator adopts a mentorship posture — walking
through reasoning explicitly, naming assumptions, explaining the
why, surfacing adjacent concerns — rather than delivering bare
conclusions.** The handoff goes both ways: the orchestrator's
explicit reasoning invites the human to correct or extend it,
often surfacing domain knowledge that then gets captured in
durable form.

A complementary requirement: the posture must be opt-out-able.
In some contexts (high-volume execution, well-understood domains,
time pressure) the human may want terse answers. The orchestrator
needs a way to detect or be told that mentorship is not wanted
in a given turn.

## Benefits

- Domain knowledge the human carries but hasn't documented gets
  surfaced and captured during the conversation, becoming future
  agents' fingertips.
- Future agents inherit a richer model of the project than what
  any single session could produce.
- The human collaborator gets educated on the system's mechanics
  in context, not via separate tutorial sessions.
- The orchestrator's reasoning becomes auditable: when it walks
  through its logic, the human can correct it before any
  irreversible action is taken.

## Challenges

- "Limited knowledge" is contextual — the orchestrator must
  calibrate without making every interaction feel like teaching.
- The opt-out mechanism is not yet defined. Options include:
  explicit human signal per turn ("don't explain, just do"),
  session-level preference, or behavioral heuristics (e.g., when
  the human's message is short and decisive, terse answers are
  preferred).
- Mentorship output adds length and latency to every turn where
  it's active. The cost is real and must be worth it.
- There's a risk of the orchestrator being patronizing or
  over-explaining obvious things. Calibrating is hard.
- The boundary between "explaining the orchestrator's reasoning"
  (always good) and "explaining the human's domain back to them"
  (potentially patronizing) is subtle.

## Process when this KTLO is picked up

When this KTLO is picked up for resolution, the work should follow
the standard project template: Socratic interview first (to clarify
the principle and its boundaries), then a DEC capturing the durable
decision, then a SPEC (if applicable), then a PLAN, then the final
product. The final product is not yet determined; candidates
include:
- An updated orchestrator role description that codifies the posture
- A `mentorship-posture` skill that other agents can load when the
  human signals they want to learn
- A hybrid: orchestrator role description + skill

The interview should also decide the opt-out mechanism.

## Exit criteria

- A durable artifact (DEC, role description update, or skill) exists
  that codifies the mentorship posture principle for the
  human-side growth loop.
- The opt-out mechanism is defined and implemented.
- The orchestrator role description reflects the posture (if that
  is where the principle lives) or cites the relevant skill (if a
  skill is created).
- Observations about what helps the agent-side growth loop
  (documentation, memory, captured domain knowledge) are noted as
  future-work ideas, even if not implemented as part of this KTLO.
